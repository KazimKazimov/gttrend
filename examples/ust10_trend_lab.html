Is the 10-year trending? Eleven readings of the same rule

A move counts as a trend only if the yield ends far enough from where it started: at least 100bp, and with the duration rule on, more for slower moves (the size needed grows with √months and with current volatility). Anything less is sideways. Six methods label history after the fact; five use only data available at the time. Methods D through G add a retracement rule or a kink penalty, so a shallow countertrend move does not break a longer trend.

Segments

The methods

A · ex-post

Breakout / reversal state machine

Sideways until the range since the last turn reaches 100bp; the trend is then backdated to the low (or high). A trend ends at its extreme when price reverses 100bp (flip to the opposite trend) or makes no new extreme for 6 months (flip to sideways).

B · ex-post

Optimal piecewise-linear fit

Dynamic programming picks breakpoints that minimise squared error plus a penalty per segment (, scaled with the threshold squared). Breaks snap to the nearby peak or trough, each piece is labelled by its endpoint move, and same-label neighbours merge.

A · real-time

State machine, no backdating

Method A's state as it would have read on the day: sideways until the 100bp breakout is actually confirmed. Same turns, later.

D · ex-post & real-time

Retracement-aware state machine

Like A, but a counter-move ends a trend only if it reaches the larger of the reversal floor and ρ × the last impulse leg. A pullback that falls short, followed by a new extreme, stays inside one trend, so −2 / +1 / −3 is a single 4pp decline. A trend with no new extreme for 12 months ends at its extreme.

E · ex-post & real-time

Kalman local-linear trend

A level-plus-slope state-space model, equivalent to an HP filter with the chosen λ. Ex-post uses the smoothed slope: turning points where it changes sign, snapped to the actual peak or trough, with shallow retracements merged. Real-time runs the D rule on the one-sided filtered level (dashed line).

F · ex-post & real-time

L1 trend filter

Fits a piecewise-linear line that trades squared error against λ × the total absolute change in slope, so kinks are rare and small bends disappear first (Kim, Koh, Boyd & Gorinevsky 2009). Turning points are where the fitted slope changes sign, snapped to the actual peak or trough. Real-time fits only the trailing 60 months at each date, keeps the endpoint, and applies the D rule to that level.

G · ex-post

Hierarchical swing merging

Starts from every monthly turn and repeatedly deletes the smallest swing that is below the larger of the reversal floor and ρ × its smaller neighbour, joining the two legs around it. It stops when every remaining swing is significant. There is no real-time version: each merge depends on the swings that come after it.

C · real-time

Rolling 12-month regression

OLS slope over the trailing 12 months × 11 gives the fitted move; ≥ +100bp is up, ≤ −100bp is down. Flips shorter than 3 months are ignored. Misses slow trends that take longer than a year to cover 100bp.
